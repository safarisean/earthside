FIELD TRANSMISSION 001 / PRESENCE

You Are Here.

Matthew 6:34 · Ephesians 5:15–16

I've spent a surprising amount of my life somewhere other than where I actually am.

At work, I'm thinking about a creative endeavor

At home, I'm thinking about work.

On vacation, I'm thinking about the conflict I had two weeks prior with one of the technicians.

And occasionally, while someone is talking directly to my face, I'm thinking about what I'm going to eat later.

It's remarkable how many places a person can visit without moving his feet.

My recent Fatherhood quest has made this particularly obvious.

You can be sitting on the floor with your child while mentally planning a workout, replaying a conversation, or calculating how many hours remain before bedtime.

Your body is there.

The rest of you has drifted off.

And children seem particularly uninterested in receiving only a portion of a person.

They want you to look at the rock.

Not glance at it.

Look at it.

They want you to hear the noise they can make with their mouth. They want you to watch them do something they've already done seventeen times.

And somewhere in the middle of these interruptions, I've started wondering whether they're actually interruptions at all.

The Trouble with Tomorrow

Jesus said not to worry about tomorrow because tomorrow will worry about itself.

I've always found that a peculiar instruction.

Tomorrow seems like exactly the sort of thing I should be worrying about.

There are bills to pay, responsibilities to manage, children to raise, and a lawn that apparently has no intention of stopping.

But Jesus wasn't telling us to abandon responsibility.

He was teaching us to trust our Father.

Worry is my attempt to predict the future. Worry has a way of convincing us that mentally living in tomorrow is the same thing as preparing for it.

It isn't.

Sometimes it's simply a way of being absent from today.

Paul David Tripp frequently writes about how our circumstances reveal what rules our hearts. I think fatherhood gives us an unusually clear view.

When I can't put my phone down, what am I afraid of missing?

When I'm irritated by an interruption, what did I believe was more important?

When I can't enjoy an ordinary/simple afternoon, what am I waiting for before I allow myself to be content?

These aren't always comfortable questions.

But they're useful ones.

A Very Short Assignment

We're only here for a little while.

And while we're here, God has given us actual people to love.

Not imaginary people who exist in our future plans.

The ones currently standing in the kitchen asking for a snack.

The ones who want to tell us a story that takes an unreasonable amount of time to reach its conclusion.

The ones who would rather have ten minutes of our undivided attention than an hour of us being vaguely nearby.

Being present isn't simply a parenting technique.

It's part of learning to love our neighbors.

And some of our nearest neighbors sleep down the hall.

Perhaps becoming more like Jesus begins, in part, with learning to pay attention to the people He's placed directly in front of us.

We are here.

For now.

And that's a pretty good place to start.

Field Question

Where does your mind tend to wander when you're with the people you love?

Earthside Practice

Spend 30 minutes with your child today without your phone, an agenda, or the need to accomplish anything.

Let them decide what matters. If it's a rock, investigate the rock.

A Prayer

Father, forgive me for being absent from the life You've given me. Teach me to trust You with tomorrow and to love the people You've placed in front of me today. Help me become a man who is truly here. Amen.
